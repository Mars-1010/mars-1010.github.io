02 / RESEARCHPROPOSED PHD RESEARCH

Negotiated autonomy,
through shared work.

An AI improves a workspace and moves a monitor its user expected to remain untouched. Understanding the goal did not authorise that change.

My research connects conversation, perceived context and useful action in a desktop/XR collaborator. Floating models preview changes; gestures mark their scope. The testable proposition is that at least 80% of room-editing grants fit three fields: visible objects or a region, a permitted operation and a duration. Conditional grants test where that representation fails.

A planned comparison tests an editable rule panel, conversational authorisation, and conversation with learned defaults, all with the same permission options. Delegation overhead counts the utterances, gestures, panel edits and time needed to authorise work.

For discussion with Prof. Can Liu: Reference-Based Manipulation locates spatial scope. EchoLadder provides reversible suggestions; this proposal adds standing grants with expiry and a history across sessions. Orality connects to a first study of authorisation speech acts.

In my image-detection capstone, I reconstructed 900 decisions from frozen outputs and coefficients, exposing class-specific trade-offs. That experience informs reconstructing an AI action against the permission in force when it occurred.

01

How grants are spoken

“Move these chairs while I am away” grants latitude; “not that one” revises it. A Wizard-of-Oz study will classify these authorisation speech acts.

02

Learn reusable defaults

For each user and object-operation class, learn whether to suggest, preview, sandbox or commit. Standing grants let these defaults reduce repeated authorisation.

03

Reconstruct change

A six-week deployment will trace how permissions change across sessions, distinguishing deliberate revision, unwanted actions and delayed discovery.
