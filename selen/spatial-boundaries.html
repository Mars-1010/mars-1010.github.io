    THE RESEARCH QUESTION

    “Not here”
    isn’t “never”.

    When someone refuses a suggestion, how much of the workspace should that refusal protect?

    In an architectural review, tracing paper holds an alternative above a drawing. The original stays intact. This study borrows that separation: a translucent preview offers a change; a visible boundary lets you decide where that kind of help should pause.

    You can inspect and change the protected object or area, revise how long the boundary lasts, and undo a confirmed change.

    Research direction ↗

    WHAT YOU CAN TRY

    One rule.
    A visible consequence.

    The prototype keeps one local boundary active. A boundary has a target, an action type and a duration. The same candidate is checked against that boundary before a preview is offered. Changing stages releases stage-specific boundaries; other boundaries remain until removed.

    Suggestions come from a fixed catalogue. Confirmed layouts and boundary changes can be undone. The event log records offers, suppression, confirmation and undo. A dismissal alone never becomes a preference.

    Built: a 3D browser interaction prototype.
    Next: Unity controller input, headset testing and user studies.

    This is a new interaction study, separate from the delivered Damo Cave installation. It has not been evaluated with participants or tested in a headset.
