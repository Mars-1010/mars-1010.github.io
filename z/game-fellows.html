HUMAN–AI CO-PLAY / 2025–ONGOING

Game Fellows.
From following to co-play.

A companion that stays alongside the player, joins the activity, and makes its support visible.

My contribution
Companion behaviour, Python/Pygame implementation, development recordings, study flow and research preparation.
Current implementation
A local controller using game-state rules, with support actions and event logs.
Research status
Working prototypes and prepared Solo/Fellow trials. No recruited-user study has been completed.

01 / THE STARTING POINT

A small shared action.

I began with rule-based following and fetching, before developing the idea into a game companion.

The early prototypes let me explore how a companion could stay close, respond to a command and participate in a shared task. After discussions with my teacher, I used a Galaga-style game as a more concrete setting for co-play.

These recordings show implemented behaviours. They motivated questions about presence, coordination and player control; they do not establish improvements in those outcomes.

02 / THE REPRODUCIBLE CONTROLLER

Make support
easy to read.

  1. 01 / FORMATION

    Stay alongside.

    Maintain a stable position beside the player.

  2. 02 / INTERCEPT

    Respond to a threat.

    Use projectile positions and velocities from the game state to move into a protective position.

  3. 03 / SUPPORT FIRE

    Coordinate an action.

    Link a bounded supporting shot to the player’s firing action.

  4. 04 / RECOVER

    Return to formation.

    Use stable behaviour states and cooldowns to limit rapid switching and repeated prompts.

Text and audio callouts accompany support actions. Prepared Solo/Fellow trials use paired random seeds and gameplay/event logging, with questionnaires covering presence, trust, usefulness and player control.

The game foundation is python-pygame-galaga by Patrick Kalkman (MIT). My work concerns the companion and the interaction/study preparation.

03 / EXPLORATION & LIMITS

What the prototype
lets me ask.

How can a companion coordinate with a person during a continuous activity, while leaving that person in control?

I also explored a screenshot-based route for AI interpretation of the game situation. The current reproducible controller uses local game-state rules; it does not run a live vision-language model.

Development profiles called “Calm”, “Balanced” and “Aggressive” are fixed behaviour settings. They do not detect the player’s emotions. Research writing and study preparation are ongoing, and benefits to player experience or performance remain untested.

For my wider research direction, this project provides experience in implementing an interactive companion and making its support inspectable during an activity. General personal AI collaboration remains a future research goal.
